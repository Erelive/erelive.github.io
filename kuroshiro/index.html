Overview

A fork, and what I added

DropSauce is a free, open-source (GPLv3) comic and novel reader for Android. When I forked it on October 14, 2026 it already had the reader, the library, extension support, tracker sync and Google Drive sync. That is its maintainers' work, not mine.

Everything under “What I built” comes from my own commits on top of that base: new features, performance work in the reader, and the unit tests that cover them. The code is Kotlin, with new screens in Jetpack Compose.

Came with DropSauce

  • Manga, webtoon and novel reader
  • Library, history and downloads
  • Mihon and LNReader extension support
  • Tracker sync with AniList, MyAnimeList, Kitsu, Shikimori and MangaBaka
  • Google Drive sync and local backups

What I built

Five pieces of work

Feature

A “what happens next” guide you can check

For a comic adapted from a novel, the series page finds the original novel, places your current chapter in it, and shows what happens next, with every claim linked to its sources.

  • Finds the source novel through the MangaUpdates, AniList and ComicK APIs. It matches automatically only when a database lists the novel as the original and the title agrees; otherwise it shows the candidates side by side with the evidence for each.
  • Maps a comic chapter to a range of novel chapters with a confidence label (Sure, Likely, Rough guess) instead of a single invented number. The reader's own correction overrides every source.
  • With an optional Claude API key, Claude writes the summary, but each claim has to quote its source. The quote is searched for in the source text, and a claim whose quote isn't found is hidden as unverified.
  • Encrypts API keys with an Android Keystore key and keeps them out of backups and sync. Spoiler requests use their own HTTP client: no cookies, HTTPS only, robots.txt and per-API rate limits respected.

  1. Gather

    Wikis, databases and reader threads are read through their APIs.

  2. Claim

    Each statement stays tied to the passage it came from.

  3. Check

    An AI-written claim must quote its source, and the quote must be found there.

  4. Label

    Confirmed, Likely, Speculation or Unverified. Unverified stays hidden by default.

Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Room, Hilt, WorkManager, Claude API

Performance

A reader that does less wasted work

After a UI overhaul, reading used noticeably more battery. I compared the reading path file by file with the last version before it, and audited every background worker, wake lock and timer in the app.

  • Stopped every page of a chapter from downloading at once when it opens, and the next chapter from preloading over mobile data. It now starts 80% of the way through, on Wi-Fi.
  • Worked out whether there is a next or previous chapter once per chapter, instead of walking the whole chapter list twice on every page turn.
  • Ended an always-on screen: “Keep screen on” now lets go after 5 minutes without a touch, so a phone put down mid-chapter goes to sleep.
  • Cut the extension update check from every app launch to at most once every 6 hours.

The audit is written up with what was and wasn't measured. These fixes follow from the code path; profiling power use on a device is the next step.

Kotlin, WorkManager, OkHttp

Feature

Chapter comments that are already loaded

Readers' comments on the chapter you just finished, from ComicK, opened from inside the reader.

  • Starts loading a chapter's comments when the reader's bars open or the last page is reached, so the sheet opens with them in place instead of loading on tap.
  • Shares a request that is already in flight between the preload and the sheet, rather than making it twice.
  • Caches which upload a chapter's comments belong to on disk for 60 days, and finds it in one request instead of two.

Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, REST APIs

Feature

Download ahead and release schedules

Offline reading that looks after itself, and a library that knows when the next chapter is due.

  • Keeps the next 1 to 5 chapters of a series downloaded while it is being read, then deletes them once they are behind the current chapter or the series has gone unread for a month. It only ever deletes what it downloaded itself.
  • Waits for Wi-Fi unless mobile-data downloads are allowed, and reuses pages the reader has already loaded.
  • Predicts release schedules (“Every Thursday · next chapter expected tomorrow”) from the dates of chapters already in the database, with no network requests.
  • Binge mode holds new-chapter notifications until 3, 5, 10 or 20 chapters have piled up, per category or per series.

Kotlin, Room, WorkManager

Design

Home and Statistics, rebuilt

New Home, Explore and Statistics screens in Jetpack Compose.

  • Statistics shows reading by hour, the last 52 weeks, a reading calendar with streaks, and reading speed over 90 days with a 7-day trend line.
  • Every section is built from one read of the reading sessions by a pure builder with no clock or database of its own, so it is unit-tested directly.
  • Chart colors are checked for contrast and for color-blind readers in light and dark, and TalkBack reads each chart's summary.
  • Home shows what to carry on with, new chapters, and how long the rest of a series takes at your own reading pace.

Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, accessibility

Also shipped

  • Anime adaptation alerts from AniList, checked 50 series per request alongside the weekly chapter check.

  • Labels for icon-only buttons that TalkBack couldn't name.
